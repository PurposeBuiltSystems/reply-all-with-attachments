Reply All with Attachments — Tutorial

Outlook silently drops attachments when you Reply All. This add-in adds a reply button that keeps them. That's the whole product — but there are a couple of things worth knowing so it behaves the way you expect.

The problem, precisely

Reply All in Outlook removes attachments from the original message. That's usually right — it stops a 40 MB deck bouncing round a thread twenty times. It's wrong whenever the attachment is the conversation: a contract everyone is marking up, a drawing under review, a form being passed along. Then you're stuck forwarding, re-adding recipients by hand, and hoping you got them all.

Tutorial — your first reply

Worked example: a six-person thread about a signed maintenance agreement, with the PDF attached to the message you're reading.

1. Open the message in Outlook.
2. On the ribbon, find the Reply All + group and click Reply All with Attachments.
You should see: a reply draft addressed to everyone the original went to, with the original attachments still attached, and your signature at the top.
3. Type your reply and press Send yourself.
Nothing sends automatically. The add-in builds the draft; you always read it and press Send. That includes checking the recipient list — it's a Reply All, so it goes to everyone the original did, which is exactly what you asked for and occasionally not what you want.

Set your signature once

These replies are created by the add-in rather than by Outlook, so Outlook's own signature doesn't apply to them. Set one here instead.

1. Click Signature in the same Reply All + ribbon group.
2. Type your signature under Your reply signature. Use Add image… for a logo or a scanned signature, then Save. Clear removes it entirely.
You should see your signature at the top of the next reply you create with this button.

About images

Your signature is stored in your mailbox settings, which Microsoft caps at 32 KB total. That is a small budget for a picture.

  • An image that already fits is stored byte for byte — untouched, exactly the file you chose.
  • One that doesn't fit is compressed to make it fit. PNG is tried first so logos and line-art keep their sharp edges and transparency; JPEG is the fallback for photographs.
If your logo looks wrong or lands in an odd spot: it was almost certainly too large and got compressed. Export it smaller — around 150 px tall is plenty for a signature — and add it again. A pre-sized PNG will come through exactly as you made it.

What this add-in does not do

It doesn'tBecause
Change Outlook's normal Reply All It adds its own button. The built-in one still strips attachments, by design — this gives you the choice rather than taking one away.
Send anything on its own Every reply is a draft you review and send.
Upload your mail anywhere There is no publisher server. It runs inside Outlook and talks only to Microsoft 365.
